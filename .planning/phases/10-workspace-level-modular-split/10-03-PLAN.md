## Trust Boundaries

The FFI boundary at `extern "C"` entry points is the only external attack surface in Phase 10's scope. Phase 6 already established the Trust Boundaries for this surface (D-A1-1 opaque `xc_func_type*`, D-A1-4 opaque `xc_func_info_type*`, D-A4-2 `catch_unwind` at every entry, D-A4-3 NULL→None mapping, D-A4-4 uninitialized-handle detection); this plan **relocates** that surface from `src/compat/` (root crate's cdylib output, if Phase 6 had configured one) to `crates/libxc-compat/src/` (libxc-compat's cdylib output) without changing the symbol set, the catch_unwind wrapping, the errno mechanism, or the panic boundary. The cdylib output filename `libxc_rs.so`/`.a`/`.dylib` is preserved (D-08).

`#[unsafe(no_mangle)] pub extern "C" fn ...` definitions in `src/compat/{raw_handle,errno,legacy_eval,...}.rs` survive `git mv` byte-identically (RESEARCH §5 line 137: "preserved literally on `git mv`"). Symbol export semantics: RESEARCH §5 lines 132-136 confirms that `#[no_mangle]` symbols defined directly inside the cdylib's source tree ARE exported on Linux/macOS/Windows; this is exactly the pattern this plan uses. The known re-export-through-rlib failure (rust-lang/rust issues #63125, #128949, #98449) does NOT apply because libxc-compat IS the cdylib AND defines all extern "C" functions in its own source — no re-export hop.
